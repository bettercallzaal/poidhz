# Feedback that recruits, not feedback that grades

**Zaal, 2026-09-22:** *"we need to better to give feedback on waht we want which is to get them
to make content for us fulltime"*.

That changes what the feedback is for. Research doc `community/2536-bounty-entrant-feedback`
optimised for **the entrant coming back and submitting something better**. That is a contest
goal. The goal here is **a working relationship with two or three people**, which is a hiring
goal, and the two want different sentences.

This document reviews the six blocks that were drafted for bounty one against the hiring goal,
names what each one gets wrong, and sets the shape that replaces them.

## The six blocks as drafted - ten defects

### 1. Not one of them asks a question

Six of six are broadcasts. A grading note can be one-way; a recruiting note exists to get a
reply, and a message that needs no answer will not get one. This is the largest single defect
and it is also the cheapest to fix.

### 2. Four of five end with the identical sentence

"Round three opens after 5pm today. It will not take posters."

If the first line is personal and the last line is a form letter, the form letter is the half
that reads as true. The closer is the position of maximum weight in a short message and it was
spent on a rule.

### 3. They say what we will not take, never what we need

"It will not take posters" is a rejection frame. To someone you want to hire, the equivalent
information is "here is what I need made next week", which is the same fact pointed forwards.

### 4. They grade the artifact and never the person

The notes describe pictures. Hiring runs on capabilities, and several entrants demonstrated
one clearly while the draft recorded only its output:

| Entrant | What the draft praised | The capability actually demonstrated |
|---|---|---|
| @predaking | eight acts in playing order | **Production discipline.** He went and got the running order and the genres. That is research, and it is the part that cannot be taught. |
| @uniquebeing404 | "shot on a real street" | **Initiative.** Eleven entries across two rounds and he is the only person who left the house. |
| @dee-13 | the artwork carries every fact | **Works in options.** She sent several versions because she did not know which was wanted. That is client behaviour. |
| @pascaline | "the composition pulls the eye" | **Speed under a deadline**, which she named herself. |
| @coolhat | "holds together square" | **Typesetting.** That much information at 1:1 is hard. |

### 5. The improvement notes are one-off fixes, not transferable skills

"Move the URL up into the body" repairs one file. "Design for the crop, because every feed
centre-crops 9:16" is carried into the next twelve pieces. Identical information; one of them
is worth having a relationship over.

### 6. Nothing says where the person stands with Zaal

Not a ranking - decision 4 of doc 2536 still holds and there is no 1-to-6 order here. A
**relationship statement** is a different object: "you are someone I would hand a brief to" is
the most motivating sentence available in this whole exercise and it appears nowhere.

### 7. The criticism is the longest paragraph in four of six

Correct for quality improvement, per Gross 2017. Inverted for recruiting: the reader finishes
knowing mostly what they got wrong, which is the right outcome for a contestant and the wrong
one for someone deciding whether to work with you.

### 8. Paid ongoing work is never named as a category

The entrants have no way to know it exists. If the goal is full time, the word has to appear.

### 9. The public comment wastes the highest-leverage surface

As drafted it is a retrospective addressed to people who already entered. It is also read by
everyone who did **not** enter, which makes it the only place the recruiting ask reaches new
people. It should carry the ask.

### 10. No route to reply

No DM invitation, no Discord, no "reply here". Even where a note wanted a conversation, it
gave no door.

## What can honestly be offered, and what cannot

Checked against the repo rather than assumed.

**CAN be offered.** Zaal's ruling, 2026-09-05, recorded in `rounds/r7/retainer-outreach.md`:
open a conversation about paid ongoing work and **ask what it would cost**. Two rules attach
to it and both are his:

1. **Never name a budget first.** "Price comes from them, not from us - the point of the
   message is to get their number, not to anchor it."
2. **Say the eligibility trade up front.** A retainer holder is not eligible for the bounty
   pot. `rounds/r7/description.md:56` already carries the live wording. Finding this out after
   signing reads as a bait and switch, so it goes in the first message.

**CANNOT be offered: distribution.** No pins, no reposts, no "we will run it on our channels".
From `docs/PROMISE-AUDIT.md`: R1 promised only the pot and kept it; **every round since has
promised distribution on top of money and every one delivered the money and not the
distribution.** R3's winner announcement is filled in, marked READY TO SEND, and has never been
posted, nearly three months late. Offering channel distribution in a recruiting message would
be making the exact promise this programme has broken five times, to the people whose goodwill
the whole idea depends on.

**The clean-slate fact, and it is worth saying out loud.** Bounty one's text
(`rounds/daily/d01/description.md:132`) reads: *"This bounty promises the pot and nothing
else."* It is the first round since R1 to promise only the pot, and the pot is paid. **So none
of these six is owed anything.** That is precisely what makes it honest to approach them now,
and it is the difference between this and the wimpydwi outreach, which is gated behind R5's
unsent winner announcement for exactly this reason.

## The replacement shape

Four lines. The first two are the craft note; the last two are the recruiting half.

1. **The capability the piece proves.** Named as a capability, not as a property of the image.
2. **The transferable skill to add.** One. Phrased so it applies to the next twelve pieces.
3. **Where they stand with you.** A relationship statement, never a rank.
4. **A question they can answer.**

Plus, on the notes that carry an offer: the price-comes-from-them ask and the eligibility
trade, in that order.

## Do not send the offer to all six

Two reasons. You cannot retain six people, and an offer that goes to everybody reads as a
mailing list, which destroys the thing it is trying to create.

So the notes split into two tiers:

- **Craft note to everyone.** Lines 1 and 2, plus the next close. Shorter than the current
  draft, not longer.
- **Conversation opener to the few.** All four lines plus the offer.

**Recommended for the offer, and this is Zaal's call, not mine:**

- **@predaking** - production discipline, credited the artists unprompted, fact-complete. The
  only correction needed is a crop habit.
- **@uniquebeing404** - owns the format nobody else touches and is the only entrant who has
  ever made something physical. The blocker is punctuality, which is a question worth asking
  rather than a reason to skip him.
- **@dee-13** - works in options, and the only thing wrong was the handover, not the work.

**Hold for one more round:** @pascaline and @coolhat. Both have a real skill and both missed
the brief in a way that matters more for paid work than for a bounty - invented people in one
case, a redrawn mark in the other. Following the brief **is** the job, so the honest move is to
say so and make the offer conditional on one clean round. Both notes below do that explicitly
rather than going quiet, which is the failure mode to avoid.

## What this does not change

- No ranking published, ever. Doc 2536 decision 4.
- No superlatives. Zaal, 2026-09-22: *"you cant say that most complete entrie didnt win"*.
- Individual notes go private; the public comment carries the winner, the thanks and the ask.
- The AI question stays out of feedback and lives in the bounty text.
